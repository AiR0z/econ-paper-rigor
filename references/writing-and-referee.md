# Writing and Referee Work

Use this reference for bounded drafting, revision, and referee reports.

## Drafting and revision

Confirm the requested section and its purpose before writing. Preserve the
project's canonical source, citation style, terminology, notation, and table or
figure numbering. Do not invent results, references, data access, robustness
checks, or author decisions.

For each paragraph, identify its job: question, prior evidence, mechanism,
design, result, limitation, or implication. Put the main claim early, connect it
to a located source or result, and remove detail that does not serve the
section. Keep causal verbs below the identification ceiling.

When revising, distinguish:

- edits that preserve meaning;
- edits that narrow or qualify a claim;
- substantive changes that require author approval or new analysis.

Never silently make the third category. Flag unresolved numbers, citations,
cross-references, and claims with an explicit marker rather than filling them by
guesswork.

## Referee report

Open with the paper's question, contribution as understood, and overall
assessment. Then organize comments by severity:

- `critical`: threatens the estimand, data validity, identification, inference,
  or central contribution;
- `major`: materially changes interpretation or requires substantial analysis;
- `minor`: improves clarity, reporting, or reproducibility without changing the
  main conclusion.

Every criticism should identify a location, explain the consequence, and give
a feasible remedy or decision point. Separate verified problems from questions
that require author clarification. Include strengths worth preserving so fixes
do not accidentally remove useful design or exposition choices.

End with a short priority order, unresolved author decisions, and the claim
ceiling the current evidence supports.
